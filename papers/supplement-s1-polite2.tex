\begin{abstract}
This supplement carries the detail the paper abridges, in its second
edition covering the complete maintenance ladder. The paper reports
abdication failover at 26--30~ms per cycle, the timeout-driven takeover
at 20{,}370~ms, the whole-cluster reboot serving at 2{,}596~ms, the
live member swap at 23{,}542~ms, and the crash-restart scenario
recorded blocked-with-evidence. Here: the exact run configuration, the
anchor tables every number is computed from, the raw log lines and
flight-tape records that witness each transition, the
client-continuity computation, the blocked scenario with its
diagnostics and its safety analysis, the session lineage (which agent
session ran what, and how the figures in this document were recomputed
from the raw artefacts rather than taken from session claims), and the
reproduction path. Every claim carries a pointer to the lab book in
\code{papers/results/} whose executable record evidences it; the lab
books are the leaf layer of the paper $\to$ supplement $\to$ lab book
$\to$ snapshot chain.
\end{abstract}

\section{The run and its configuration}
\label{sec:config}

The run is \code{local-polite2-2026-09-19}: a three-node
\code{lunet-locks} cluster on one host (macOS, loopback), calm
configuration so the baseline churn of the tight-timeout cells is
absent --- heartbeat 5~ms, election timeout 3{,}000~ms (sloppy detector
3{,}000--5{,}000~ms), recovery 3{,}000~ms. The binary is the
flight-recorder build at commit \code{7ff3f12}; every node's tape was
growing before the scenarios began. Load throughout: one
\code{lease-load} client per node, polite cadence, roughly 17
operations per second across the cluster (measured: 432/442/456 lease
operations at n1/n3/n2 over the 24.7~s S2 window).

The ladder, in order: \textbf{S0} steady serving (240~s) $\to$
\textbf{S1} security-patch rotation through the non-leaders
(SIGTERM/stop/restart, one node at a time) $\to$ \textbf{S2} leader
abdication cycled three times so every node holds leadership once
$\to$ \textbf{S3} leader SIGTERM/restart (the timeout-driven takeover
baseline against S2) $\to$ \textbf{S4} SIGKILL crashes, leader last
$\to$ \textbf{S5} full-cluster stop/start $\to$ \textbf{S6} controlled
reconfiguration swap (explicit remove+add).

The campaign ran three times. Run A (snapshot 06:10:26Z) aborted at
its steady gate: 26 client errors, traced to two host-side bugs in the
run's own custom stream client (a malformed GET line and a message-id
generator with only 16 distinct rotations) plus one 18~s all-server
timeout under investigation. Run B (snapshot 06:12:40Z) completed S0,
S1, and S2 --- the numbers of Section~\ref{sec:s2} --- and stopped at
S3 for a harness reason: the serving probe was single-shot where the
gate needed retry-to-deadline, and S4's rejoin budgets were below the
measured era-fold walk (20--56~s). Run C, with both gates fixed,
completed S3 en route to S4 at the time of writing
(Section~\ref{sec:s3}). Each run's full artefact set was snapshotted
by \code{snapshot\_run} (logs, superblock images, flight tapes,
anchors, progress log) before the run directory was wiped; the three
archives are the evidence base of record.

\section{S0: steady state}
\label{sec:s0}

240~s under load, anchor \ts{steady-serving-begin} at
\ts{1789798363020} (run B): zero client errors; all three nodes
\code{normal:v0:leader=n1} at the window's end; no view change
emitted. This is the calm baseline the tight-timeout softball cells
never had: no suspicion ever fired.

\section{S2: abdication, measured end to end}
\label{sec:s2}

\subsection{What is being measured}

The paper's claim under test: failover driven by abdication is
detection-independent --- bounded by the proposal round-trip, not by
any timeout --- because the abdication itself proposes the view
change. The measured quantity per cycle is the span from the operator
verb's reply (\code{S2-abdicate-drive},
\code{reply=\{"action":"abdicate","accepted":true\}}) to the
installation of the successor view's first commit, plus the settle
span to all three nodes reporting \code{normal} in the new view.

\subsection{The abdicating leader's return}

Each cycle's settled states show the abdicating leader back
\code{normal} in the successor view within the settle time
(150--181~ms): it stops accepting new operations at the abdication,
the successor view installs, and the old leader rejoins as an ordinary
member. No node left the cluster; no announcement, no forced sequence,
no weight change participated.

\section{S3: the timeout-driven baseline}
\label{sec:s3}

The completed ladder's S3 row (leader n1 SIGTERM'd; the runner's
measure line verbatim): stop 50~ms; the takeover --- successor n2
elected and serving --- at 20{,}370~ms; n1's rejoin to \code{normal}
at 42{,}991~ms. The takeover spans the sloppy detector's window
(3{,}000--5{,}000~ms) plus the era-fold walk the new leader's catch-up
required; the rejoin tail matches the S1 shape (churn accumulated
during absence, walked at the harness cadence; lab book
\code{02-abdication-failover.md}, ``the contrast'').

The contrast is the paper's point: the same leadership change costs
26--30~ms when the leader abdicates and 20.4~s when the cluster must
detect its death --- three orders of magnitude apart on identical
hardware and knobs.

The softball campaign's attempt-3 row (tight timeouts, heartbeat
5~ms, election 1{,}000~ms, leader SIGTERM): successor elected
correctly; the restarted leader's rejoin took approximately 78~s
against a 30~s gate --- the boot-fenced chase of a churning view
space, the failure class the paper's E1 analyses.

\section{S4: the crash-restart scenario, blocked with evidence}
\label{sec:s4}

The ladder's S4 SIGKILL'd all three nodes in turn (non-leaders first,
the leader last), restarting each on the same boot line. The boot-gate
classification worked on every crash: each reboot read its fence,
found no clean-stop marker, and bumped its identity into the high
band (old identities 1/2/3, new \code{own=16777217/16777218/16777219},
\code{incarnation=1}; witness lines
\code{papers/results/data/s4-bump-witness.txt}). Each bumped node's
first act was the reincarnation announcement to all three peers in the
millisecond of its boot (its flight tape opens with the host's
\code{reincarnate} drive and the tag-\code{0x0d} frames;
\code{papers/results/data/s4-bumped-tape-head.txt}).

The rejoin walk then wedged, on all three crashes. The leader's log
names the refusal: \code{ReincarnationRefused \{ sender: NodeId(2),
\ldots\}} --- the announcement arriving attributable to the OLD
identity where the bumped node announced from the NEW one; the
harness's transport identity mapping sits between the two, and the
engine declines to reconfigure on a message it cannot attribute ---
the safe direction to fail, repeated 686 times across the three
nodes' logs (227/282/177 at n1/n3/n2; samples
\code{papers/results/data/s4-refusal-samples.txt}). The forced walk
never started (\code{remap} notices at zero peers), the bumped nodes
sat fenced for the full 150~s budget, and the serving pair's views
stormed (1{,}431 and 1{,}146 leader-change records across the run on
n1 and n3 --- the era-fold walk hunting a shape the refused
announcement never gave it; lab book
\code{03-crash-reincarnation.md}, ``where the rig stopped'').

\textbf{The safety statement, checked on the tapes.} The ladder
closed with \code{total\_client\_errors=0}: no committed operation
was lost anywhere in the run. No superseded identity voted again ---
the refusals are the protocol declining to act. The defect is a
liveness defect of the harness's rejoin walk, host-side by the
repository's bug-provenance ruling, localised to the transport
identity mapping; the repair is the downstream repository's active
work. The 2$\times$RTT crash-reincarnation floor the paper claims is
the protocol's, evidenced per piece (classification, bump,
announcement, and the fused-reconfiguration machinery measured healthy
in S6's 28~ms decrement round), with this end-to-end block named.

\section{S5: the whole-cluster reboot}
\label{sec:s5}

Two passes, both recorded. Pass 1 attempted the durable resurrection
of the post-S4 state --- three nodes holding divergent retained views
from the view storms (one at view 1{,}383, the others fenced at 0):
the cluster did not converge in 120~s (all three \code{restarting
v0}, 0/3 voters; the same era-fold family as Section~\ref{sec:s4}).
Pass 2 did what an operator does: snapshot for the record,
re-provision fresh, reboot. All down 30~s; all up; all three voters
\code{normal} at 2{,}578~ms and the first client operation served at
2{,}596~ms (anchors \code{papers/results/data/s5-anchors.txt}; lab
book \code{04-reboot-and-reconfig.md}).

\section{S6: the controlled reconfiguration swap}
\label{sec:s6}

n3 was retired and n4 joined, live, as four committed reconfiguration
rounds: decrement 28~ms (one round-trip --- the same machinery as the
abdication), leave 5{,}022~ms, join 5{,}017~ms, increment 5{,}026~ms
(the 5-second rounds are the harness's drive cadence between rounds);
the swap completed in 23{,}542~ms with n4 serving at voting weight
11{,}383~ms in, the cluster serving throughout (the measure line
verbatim in lab book \code{04}; anchors
\code{papers/results/data/s6-anchors.txt}).

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The run's own REPORT discloses, and this supplement repeats: the
per-operation stream client (softball7's shape) was dropped from the
run after a scratch tooling probe of that shape lost replies
intermittently ($\sim$4\% per attempt) against this rig on this
host, while the shipped Rust clients did not; the tape shows the
wedged operations committing at the leader with the reply emitted ---
the loss is in the scratch client's connection handling, not the
cluster, and the runner replaced it with the shipped
\code{lease-client} serving probes. The load clients' 5~s op-timeout
tail (exactly three samples per client, in the S4/S5 outage windows)
sits in the cumulative p99/max; the clean-window medians carry the
mid-distribution. And Section~\ref{sec:s4}'s block stands as the
ladder's principal incomplete scenario.

\section{Reproduction}
\label{sec:repro}

The ladder is a single command against the committed runner:
\code{luajit examples/lease-sequencer/run-polite2.lua}, which boots
the calm configuration, drives the scenarios in order, writes the
anchor and progress logs, and snapshots the run directory at the end.
The knobs are verbatim in \code{anchors.md}'s header (heartbeat 5~ms,
election 3{,}000~ms, recovery 3{,}000~ms). The figures of this
supplement are recomputed from the snapshots by the method of
Section~\ref{sec:provenance}: anchors give the spans; the raw
\code{lease-attempt} and \code{leader} log lines give the wire
witnesses; the flight tapes give the commit stamps.
