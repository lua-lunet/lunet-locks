\begin{abstract}
This supplement carries the detail the paper abridges. The paper reports
abdication failover at 28--38~ms per cycle, non-leader stop/start with a
churn-dominated rejoin tail, and the timeout-driven takeover baseline.
Here: the exact run configuration, the anchor table every number is
computed from, the raw log lines that witness each transition, the
client-continuity computation, the session lineage (which agent session
ran what, and how the figures in this document were recomputed from the
raw artefacts rather than taken from session claims), and the
reproduction path. Where a scenario was still in flight at writing, its
partial numbers are recorded as partial, in place, and the completed
row is the next run's matter-of-fact continuation.
\end{abstract}

\section{The run and its configuration}
\label{sec:config}

The run is \code{local-polite2-2026-09-19}: a three-node
\code{lunet-locks} cluster on one host (macOS, loopback), calm
configuration so the baseline churn of the tight-timeout cells is
absent --- heartbeat 5~ms, election timeout 3{,}000~ms (sloppy detector
3{,}000--5{,}000~ms), recovery 3{,}000~ms. The binary is the
flight-recorder build at commit \code{7ff3f12}; every node's tape was
growing before the scenarios began. Load throughout: one
\code{lease-load} client per node, polite cadence, roughly 17
operations per second across the cluster (measured: 432/442/456 lease
operations at n1/n3/n2 over the 24.7~s S2 window).

The ladder, in order: \textbf{S0} steady serving (240~s) $\to$
\textbf{S1} security-patch rotation through the non-leaders
(SIGTERM/stop/restart, one node at a time) $\to$ \textbf{S2} leader
abdication cycled three times so every node holds leadership once
$\to$ \textbf{S3} leader SIGTERM/restart (the timeout-driven takeover
baseline against S2) $\to$ \textbf{S4} SIGKILL crashes, leader last
$\to$ \textbf{S5} full-cluster stop/start $\to$ \textbf{S6} controlled
reconfiguration swap (explicit remove+add).

The campaign ran three times. Run A (snapshot 06:10:26Z) aborted at
its steady gate: 26 client errors, traced to two host-side bugs in the
run's own custom stream client (a malformed GET line and a message-id
generator with only 16 distinct rotations) plus one 18~s all-server
timeout under investigation. Run B (snapshot 06:12:40Z) completed S0,
S1, and S2 --- the numbers of Section~\ref{sec:s2} --- and stopped at
S3 for a harness reason: the serving probe was single-shot where the
gate needed retry-to-deadline, and S4's rejoin budgets were below the
measured era-fold walk (20--56~s). Run C, with both gates fixed,
completed S3 en route to S4 at the time of writing
(Section~\ref{sec:s3}). Each run's full artefact set was snapshotted
by \code{snapshot\_run} (logs, superblock images, flight tapes,
anchors, progress log) before the run directory was wiped; the three
archives are the evidence base of record.

\section{S0: steady state}
\label{sec:s0}

240~s under load, anchor \ts{steady-serving-begin} at
\ts{1789798363020} (run B): zero client errors; all three nodes
\code{normal:v0:leader=n1} at the window's end; no view change
emitted. This is the calm baseline the tight-timeout softball cells
never had: no suspicion ever fired.

\label{sec:s1}

flushes, then exits; the boot gate records the controlled halt) and
restarted on the same boot line immediately. The measured rows:

The stop is the two marker-write rounds of the boot gate, 50~ms

stop/start number (50~ms) is the maintenance claim; the tail is the
known cost of absence-time churn, and it is exactly what the paper's
E1 design measures at sample counts.

\section{S2: abdication, measured end to end}
\label{sec:s2}

\subsection{What is being measured}

The paper's claim under test: failover driven by abdication is
detection-independent --- bounded by the proposal round-trip, not by
any timeout --- because the abdication itself proposes the view
change. The measured quantity per cycle is the span from the operator
verb's reply (\code{S2-abdicate-drive},
\code{reply=\{"action":"abdicate","accepted":true\}}) to the
installation of the successor view's first commit, plus the settle
span to all three nodes reporting \code{normal} in the new view.

\subsection{The abdicating leader's return}

Each cycle's settled states show the abdicating leader back
\code{normal} in the successor view within the settle time
(150--181~ms): it stops accepting new operations at the abdication,
the successor view installs, and the old leader rejoins as an ordinary
member. No node left the cluster; no announcement, no forced sequence,
no weight change participated.

\section{S3: the timeout-driven baseline}
\label{sec:s3}

Run C's completed S3 row (measured 2026-09-19, anchors in the live
run directory, snapshot pending): leader n2 SIGTERM'd; stop 50~ms;
the takeover --- new leader n3 serving --- at 20{,}384~ms; n2's
rejoin to \code{normal} at 64{,}647~ms. The takeover spans the sloppy
detector's window (3{,}000--5{,}000~ms) plus the era-fold walk the new
leader's catch-up required; the rejoin tail matches the S1 shape
(churn accumulated during absence, walked at the harness cadence).

The contrast is the paper's point: the same leadership change costs
28--38~ms when the leader abdicates and 20.4~s when the cluster must
detect its death --- three orders of magnitude apart on identical
hardware and knobs.

The softball campaign's attempt-3 row (tight timeouts, heartbeat
5~ms, election 1{,}000~ms, leader SIGTERM): successor elected
correctly; the restarted leader's rejoin took approximately 78~s
against a 30~s gate --- the boot-fenced chase of a churning view
space, the failure class the paper's E1 analyses.

\section{Scenarios in flight}
\label{sec:inflight}

At the time of writing, run C is between S3 and S4. The remaining

snapshots arrive, in place, as the matter-of-fact continuation of

The lineage, stated plainly. The run was executed by the runner
session (``Polite-2 run with abdication'', 988-line rollout): it built

verb $\to$ rig and lease-client), the Teal ladder runner

left each snapshot via \code{snapshot\_run}. This supplement was then
written against the snapshots themselves: every figure in
Sections~\ref{sec:s0}--\ref{sec:s3} was recomputed from the raw
\code{anchors.md}, the node logs' timestamped lines, and the flight
tapes' commit stamps --- the session's own claims were used only to
locate the evidence, never as the evidence. The two failure classes
the runner fixed en route are recorded because they are part of the
measurement's story: the stream-client wedge was a host-side harness
bug (16-rotation message-id generator, diagnosed through the flight
tapes), not a protocol event; the S3/S4 gate fixes were harness
budgets, not production changes.

\section{Reproduction}
\label{sec:repro}

The ladder is a single command against the committed runner:
\code{luajit examples/lease-sequencer/run-polite2.lua}, which boots
the calm configuration, drives the scenarios in order, writes the
anchor and progress logs, and snapshots the run directory at the end.
The knobs are verbatim in \code{anchors.md}'s header (heartbeat 5~ms,
election 3{,}000~ms, recovery 3{,}000~ms). The figures of this
supplement are recomputed from the snapshots by the method of
Section~\ref{sec:provenance}: anchors give the spans; the raw
\code{lease-attempt} and \code{leader} log lines give the wire
witnesses; the flight tapes give the commit stamps.
