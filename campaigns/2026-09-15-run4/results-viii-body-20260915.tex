\section{Experimental results}\label{sec:experiments}

All numbers in this section are from the 2026-09-15 campaign on the
three-node voting configuration of the \code{lunet-locks} demonstration
(Section~\ref{sec:demo}): three voting replicas --- ids 44, 55, 66, one
per Scaleway datacentre (\code{pl-waw-1}, \code{pl-waw-2},
\code{pl-waw-3}), each on a \code{DEV1-S} virtual machine --- each
accompanied by a weight-0 telemetry standby (ids 77, 88, 99) in the
same region, and one polite lease client per datacentre (ids 800002,
800004, 800006) contending for a single lock, id 14531090, at a 500~ms
lease window renewed at the half-window point, with a 1000~ms floor on
non-holder probes. Every host runs chrony against the same four Google
NTP sources with \code{makestep 1.0 3} and \code{rtcsync}; measured
system offsets at run start were 18--46~\si{\micro\s} across the three
datacentres, so every elapsed time below is a cross-host difference
between host wall clocks that agree to well under a tenth of a
millisecond. Each anchor is \code{date +\%s\%3N} stamped on the acting
host at the moment the pause took effect; elapsed times are always
$T+\Delta$ from that anchor, never raw epochs.

\subsection{E1: the polite chase --- one holder, a renewal metronome}

The load gate starts silent and the campaign releases it per client
(SIGUSR2); a contender's first action is a GET probe, never a blind
renewal. The observed discipline across the whole healthy window: the
holder renews at the half-window point and holds until the experiment
pauses it; the two non-holders probe at the 1000~ms floor and never
race a live lease. One holder exists at every instant; every refusal
the clients see is a leader decision they absorb and back off from.

The client-side operation latencies split cleanly by leader locality:
the holder whose datacentre hosts the leader commits renewals at
p50 2.9~ms (local UDP peer), while holders in the other two
datacentres --- whose verbs the non-leader voters forward to the
leader --- commit at p50 45--52~ms with p99 $\le$ 66~ms. The verb
forwarding path costs one cross-datacentre hop and no more.

\subsection{E2: anchored takeover under holder pause}

The experiment pauses the acting holder's client process on its own
host (SIGUSR1 on the gate), stamps the anchor on that host's clock,
and watches the committed stream for the successor's acquire. Every
pause of the acting holder produced exactly one successor acquire and
no conflicting holder at any instant.

The takeover latencies land where the design puts them: the lease
expires 500~ms after the paused holder's last renewal, and the first
non-holder probe past the expiry takes the lock 52--71~ms later ---
one probe race plus one leader RTT. The polite takeover bound is the
lease window by construction, and the measurements sit inside it.

\subsection{E3: the campaign's hardening results}

The rig campaign is itself a result stream: three correctness breaks
were found, root-caused on live clusters, and closed with
replay-shaped defensive tests on the exact failing shapes.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{The descriptor is a hint list, not membership law.} A
\code{--name} the cluster descriptor omits boots as a weight-0 joining
member whose identity comes from \code{--join-id} and
\code{--join-endpoint}, appended after the hint rows; the node stays
fenced until the leader's committed configuration carries its row (the
join verb at the leader is the act of entry; a future PSK, not this
file, is the cross-environment boundary). The previous
\code{--name not in descriptor} boot refusal is gone.
\item \emph{The wire's identity discipline.} Every request id a client
puts on the wire --- the holder of a lease candidate, the message id of
every verb --- is the canonical hyphenated lowercase UUID form,
refused loudly at decode otherwise, before the request can enter the
replication log; the client asserts the same form at its identity draw
and on both sides of the reply comparison. The break this closes: a
bare-hex identity drew, parsed silently, and missed its own granted
renewal in the comparison, so no tenure survived --- the unit tests of
the day generated fixtures in whatever form the code under test
produced, which is precisely why the wire now refuses the wrong form
instead of trusting the tests.
\item \emph{Fault observability at the self-arrest.} A faulted node
poisons itself and stops serving (sticky by design, the fail-stop
contract), and the first fault observation now records and publishes
its reason --- the publish-gate breach, the journal refusal, the
boundary panic --- in the node status and over a dedicated fault
query ABI, instead of leaving a silently stopped service. The
campaign's collapse window (E4) was diagnosed to its mechanism in one
pass because the adapter half answered ``why''; before the change it
answered nothing.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{E4: the view-change collapse --- an upstream breach}

The campaign's third pause window exposed a genuine upstream defect,
reported here as fact. At kill\#3's takeover --- which committed
cleanly --- the cluster executed its first view change of the run, 7
minutes after bring-up, with the weight-0 telemetry learners joined.
From that instant: the incumbent leader (id 55) left the protocol and
never appeared again; the two surviving voters exchanged view changes
at roughly six per second (views 15 through 510+) with \emph{zero}
committed slots; the last committed operations were the takeover
acquire plus two probe replies 3.8~s after the anchor; the verb path
went silent cluster-wide --- the followers' forwarders waiting on a
leader that no longer commits --- while the heartbeat and telemetry
planes kept flowing. Each voter eventually arrested itself into its
poisoned fail-stop state; with the observability change (E3.3) the
arrest now names its reason.

The vendored \code{vrr-core} is at upstream \code{origin/main}
(\code{058acdc}); the breach reproduces in the vendor tree's own
learner-acquisition corpus class, and no fix exists upstream at this
writing. The kill\#3 window and everything after it are excluded from
the campaign's measurements (the client-side error counts in that
window are the collapse's own artifacts, not lease behaviour); the
healthy prefix --- the sustain of E1 and the takeovers of E2 --- is
untouched by it and is what the tables above report.

\subsection{Held verdicts}

On a three-region commodity cloud with synchronised clocks: (a) the
polite lease chase holds exactly one holder at a time with a renewal
cadence that is a 25~ms-wide band around the configured half-window;
(b) pausing the acting holder hands the lock to the next datacentre's
client in a measured 552--571~ms, inside the lease window by
construction; (c) verb forwarding costs one cross-datacentre hop
(p50 45--52~ms for a remote holder, p99 $\le$ 66~ms); and (d) the
campaign found and closed three client- and adapter-side correctness
gaps with replay-shaped defensive tests, and surfaced one upstream
view-change breach with weight-0 learners --- the vendored core's
own acquisition corpus class --- now observable at the fault line.
